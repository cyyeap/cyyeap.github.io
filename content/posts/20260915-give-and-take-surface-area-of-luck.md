I've been in Shanghai for more than 2 weeks now. It's been a surprise. I've learnt a lot. It was quite a sentimental trip at first. I was extremely grateful that I can understand and communicate in Mandarin; it was a bit different from the last four trips I've made to China.

I first came to China in 2016, to Beijing, when my then partner was doing his mandatory overseas medical student exchange at Peking University. We were quite poor then, and my traveling style was mostly cheapskate: we stayed at a very cramped Airbnb, sharing a room with the hosts and a big dog. It was winter and it's probably some of the coldest weather I have endured, and I was wearing the quintessential Melbourne Macpac jacket, made for 10°C weather. It was so cold in Beijing, and I caught the flu. I don't even remember how I paid for things when I was in China. Back then, foreigners probably couldn't get Alipay, so I probably paid for most things in cash. I recall facing a bit of culture shock with northern food. I was sick and didn't really enjoy the trip that much, but I thought the Great Wall of China was so magnificent, and it's one of the best places I've visited.

It was such an ordeal to visit China back then, as I had to apply for a Chinese visa, hence it was always the last place on my list. I visited Taiwan and Japan 4-5 times each as a fallback when I couldn't think of a place to visit after my mandatory yearly home visit.

Since 2024, the Chinese government has made it a lot easier for foreigners to visit China by allowing a one-way visa-free policy. Since then, I've been visiting China once a year. I visited Chengdu, Chongqing, Guangzhou, Nanjing, Hangzhou, then Shanghai… which was the first Chinese city I fell in love with. When I first arrived, we stayed in the former French Concession. As a daydreamer, I told Aidan that we should probably buy a place in Shanghai; it's the second city after London that I felt I could settle down in. It has a lot of character, and culturally I felt at home. However, I changed my mind towards the end of the trip because I missed home, particularly my elderly pets, as we were away for about 3 weeks.

Originally, I was supposed to arrive about a week from now, just to show Aidan's parents around China for the first time - Beijing, Xi'an and Chengdu. Then I found out that Pet Fair Asia was happening in Shanghai in mid-August. I decided to come earlier because I've been interested in starting a business in the pet industry for many years. As you know, most youngsters would rather be pet parents than human parents.

So I'd have 3 weeks alone in China with nothing planned. I'm comfortable with this type of travelling; planning far in advance would actually stress me out. Wonderful things always came up when my trips were unplanned. I made lifelong friendships, heard interesting stories, and learnt things I otherwise would not have learnt. I thought that since I'd been to Shanghai before, I'd probably get bored after a few days. Nope, I extended my stay four times, and I moved between 4-5 hotels.

I visited Suzhou hoping to see the I.M. Pei-designed Suzhou Museum. (Most people hope to visit all the Disneylands; I hope to see all the I.M. Pei-designed buildings - ironically, I don't actually like the insides of the buildings.) After visiting Suzhou, I retreated back to Shanghai. Originally I planned to visit other, more exotic cities with a friend who is also on a career break from Melbourne, but we decided it was too hot to go anywhere due to El Niño. The weather here is not very uncomfortable.

After the pet fair, I was a bit lost on what to do. I was worried, and the weather was so unpleasant. I figured I should set at least one goal for this trip, and came up with this: learn as much as possible about entrepreneurship.

The easiest way to learn about entrepreneurship is by immersing myself, and talking to people who have done it better than me. In two weeks, I've talked to about 10 people in depth and asked them questions about being business owners. The process isn't that important. These are some of the people I've talked to:

- a serial innovator and inventor, who created a sleeker version of the Kindle Scribe and the Little Robot
- a multifaceted businesswoman who has three businesses: sustainable energy and a robotic writer, a VC middleperson service, and an ESG consultancy
- a feminist bookstore owner in Suzhou, who left her job in Beijing
- a venture capitalist who invested for Jack Ma's foundation and many Chinese high-net-worth individuals
- a leading lawyer in China who brought me to meet his friends, who are also entrepreneurs and shockingly high achievers

Write more, and use my time more wisely: that's what I have in mind for my life.
